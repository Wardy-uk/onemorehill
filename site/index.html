Ordnance Survey trig pillars cross-referenced against every named hill in Britain and Ireland. Free to use, and free to correct.

—

trig pillars stand within ten metres of a named summit. Climb one and you have bagged both — a Munro, a Wainwright, an Ethel, a Marilyn.

The trig network was built on sightlines: each pillar sited so its neighbours could be seen from it. Triangulation is a graph of lines between points.

Yet the two databases have never been joined. The hill database holds no pillars; the trig archive holds no hill lists. These are those lines.

Coverage by challenge

Share of each list with a pillar within fifty metres of the summit. The Ethels are the best-served challenge in Britain; the Wainwrights are among the worst.

The Full House — every Munro, Munro Top, Corbett, Fiona, Donald and Furth — is officially 1,072 summits. Only 1,042 are distinct hills. Thirty sit in two lists at once. Thirty of those walks count twice, and nothing tells you which.

Separations are straight lines in OSGB grid metres between the pillar's recorded position and the recorded summit. Both carry survey error; under two metres means "the same place", not a measurement. Destroyed pillars excluded — 1,030 of the 7,111 on record.

A pillar is often not on the summit. Helvellyn's stands 89 m away. They are listed here as two places, because they are two places.
