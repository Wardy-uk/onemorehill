Trig pillars on named hills

The first cross-reference of the two datasets

Britain has — standing trig pillars. — of them stand within ten metres of a named hill — a Munro, a Wainwright, an Ethel, a Marilyn. Climb one and you have bagged both.

Nobody had published which. The hill database holds no trig points; the trig archive holds no hill lists. The overlap has been invisible for as long as both have existed. So I joined them.

IIHow well served is each challenge?

Proportion of each list with a pillar within fifty metres of the summit. The Ethels are the best-served challenge in Britain. The Wainwrights are among the worst.

The Full House — every Munro, Munro Top, Corbett, Fiona, Donald and Furth — is officially 1,072 summits. Only 1,042 are distinct hills. Thirty sit in two lists at once: Ben Cleuch is a Fiona and a Donald, Merrick a Corbett and a Donald. Thirty of those walks count twice, and nothing tells you which.

Distances are straight lines in OSGB grid metres between the pillar's recorded position and the recorded summit. Both carry their own survey error, so treat anything under two metres as “the same place” rather than a measurement. Destroyed pillars are excluded — 1,030 of the 7,111 in the archive.

A pillar is frequently not on the summit. Helvellyn's stands 89 metres away. They are listed here as separate places, because they are.
